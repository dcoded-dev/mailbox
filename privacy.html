Mailbox Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Mailbox is an email client that you run on your own computer. This policy explains what data Mailbox accesses, how it is used and stored, and who it is shared with. In short: your mail stays on your computer, and the developer never receives it.

Data Mailbox accesses

When you connect a Google account with Sign in with Google, Mailbox requests these permissions:

  • Gmail (https://mail.google.com/): to download your messages, labels and attachments; to mark messages read or unread, star, archive, label and delete them; and to send mail you write.
  • Basic profile (openid, email, profile): to show which account is connected, using your email address and name.

If you connect an account with a Google app password instead, Mailbox accesses the same Gmail data using that password.

How the data is used

Mailbox uses your Google data only to provide its email features to you: showing, searching, organizing and sending your mail, and keeping it in sync with Gmail. It does not use your data for advertising. It does not sell your data. It does not use your data to train AI or machine-learning models.

Where the data is stored

  • Your mail, search index and settings are stored in a database on the computer where you run Mailbox.
  • Google sign-in tokens (or app passwords) are stored in that same local database, so Mailbox can stay connected without asking you to sign in again.
  • If you open Mailbox on your phone, the phone keeps a cache of recent messages in its browser storage so you can read and search them offline.

The developer does not operate any server that receives your mail, tokens or account information.

Sharing

Mailbox shares your data only in these cases, all of which you control:

  • Google: Mailbox communicates with Gmail's servers to sync and send your mail.
  • AI assistants you connect: if you choose to connect an AI assistant (such as Claude) to Mailbox through the Model Context Protocol, that assistant can search, read, organize and send mail when you ask it to. What it receives is then governed by that assistant provider's own privacy policy. This feature is off until you set it up.
  • Your own devices: when you open Mailbox from your phone or another device you control.

No data is shared with the developer or any other third party.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Mailbox's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • Mailbox uses Google user data only to provide the email features you see in the app.
  • It transfers that data only as needed to provide those features (to Gmail, to your own devices, and to an AI assistant only if you connect one and ask it to act on your mail), or as required by law.
  • It does not use or transfer the data for advertising, and does not sell it.
  • No person reads your data. The developer has no access to it, because it never leaves your devices except to go to Google or to services you choose.
  • It does not use the data to develop, improve or train generalized or non-personalized AI or machine-learning models.

Retention and deletion

  • Removing an account in Mailbox (Manage accounts → Remove) deletes that account's mail and tokens from your computer.
  • Deleting Mailbox's data folder deletes all stored mail, tokens and settings.
  • You can revoke Mailbox's access to your Google account at any time at myaccount.google.com/connections.

Security

Mailbox listens only on your own computer by default and is protected by a passcode. Remote access from other devices is meant to go through a private network you control, such as Tailscale. Email content is displayed in a sandbox with scripts disabled, and remote images are blocked until you allow them.

Children

Mailbox is not directed at children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new date.
